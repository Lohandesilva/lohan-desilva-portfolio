S&P 500 profitability and valuation

Index fundamentals rebuilt from accounting identities: where profitability actually varies, and what the market pays for it.

Case study Repository Executive summary

Sector explains only 26% of the variation in profitability — three-quarters of it sits inside sectors, not between them. And once size is controlled for, the market pays less for a higher margin, not more.

Margin dispersion within sectors

Median dot, interquartile box, p10-p90 whisker. The width of each row is the finding: sector medians differ, but the spread inside a sector is wider still.

Source: 354 constituents with a full derived income statement. Financials and Real Estate margins are not comparable to the rest.

The market pays for size, not margin

Every constituent, sized by market capitalisation. Higher-margin companies do not command higher multiples.

Source: EBITDA margin against price-to-earnings. Regression in the case study: margin coefficient −0.607, p = 0.002.

Return on equity against the multiple

The covariance behind the price-to-book decomposition: the market marks down the multiple on high-return companies.

Priced above what they can fund

Companies whose implied growth exceeds what retained earnings can pay for. A starting list for scrutiny, not a conclusion.
